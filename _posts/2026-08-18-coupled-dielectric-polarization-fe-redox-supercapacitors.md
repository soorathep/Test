Oxide heterostructures are often described as “synergistic” after they outperform either parent material. That word does not identify which phase stores charge, which phase changes the interface, or whether the proposed mechanism survives a measurement made while the electrode is operating.

Our new article in *Journal of Alloys and Compounds* asks a narrower question: can a redox-active oxide and a dielectric oxide perform different, measurable functions in the same aqueous supercapacitor electrode?

The work couples bismuth ferrite with Ta₂O₅ and follows both Fe and Ta under operating conditions. The result is deliberately evidence-bounded: the heterostructure performs well, but its mechanism is assigned only as far as the measurements allow.

## Two phases, two proposed functions

Diffraction, spectroscopy, and microscopy confirm a two-phase Ta₂O₅/bismuth ferrite heterostructure with direct physical contact. Pellet impedance resolves Maxwell-Wagner interfacial and space-charge polarization in the parent materials and the composite, with the composite showing the lowest dielectric loss over more than three decades of frequency.

The working interpretation separates functions that are usually assigned to one phase:

> **Bismuth ferrite** supplies Fe-centred, surface or near-surface redox capacity.
>
> **Ta₂O₅** contributes interfacial polarization, accessible heterointerfaces, and structural integration rather than an observed Ta-centred redox reaction.

That division of labour is a mechanistic proposal constrained by spectroscopy—not a conclusion inferred from capacitance alone.

## What operando XAS does—and does not—show

Once all seventeen operando Fe K-edge spectra are placed on a common normalization, no change in the bulk-averaged Fe oxidation state is resolved. The two-sigma bound on the reversible component corresponds to less than **1.4% of the charge stored in one cycle** across the illuminated volume.

This does not exclude Fe redox confined to the outer few nanometres of a particle. Surface-sensitive XPS resolves mixed Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺ character, so near-surface Fe redox remains consistent with the evidence, but the operando measurement does not demonstrate it directly.

At the Ta L₃-edge, the white line remains at the Ta⁵⁺ end of the reference interval in every measured state, and the Ta-O first shell is unchanged. No Ta-centred formal redox transformation is therefore assigned. At the same time, pristine Ta₂O₅ stores far more charge than its measured surface area could support as a simple double layer; the process responsible remains unresolved rather than being given a convenient label.

> The mechanism is bounded by measurement, not asserted from performance.

## Read through Coordination–Transport–Interface

The observations map naturally onto the CTI framework:

- **Coordination:** operando XAS constrains the oxidation state and first-shell response of both cations; it does not resolve a bulk redox process at either Fe or Ta.
- **Transport:** increased surface area and accessible pore volume improve electrolyte access, while the kinetic analysis shows that readily accessible and slower charge-storage processes coexist.
- **Interface:** direct oxide-oxide contact, enhanced low-frequency polarization, and lower apparent interfacial resistance are consistent with a heterogeneous interface that couples near-surface redox capacity to charge accumulation.

The composite delivers **156 F g⁻¹ at 1 mV s⁻¹**. A symmetric two-electrode device reaches **34 Wh kg⁻¹ at 558 W kg⁻¹** and retains **82%** of its capacitance after **10,000 cycles**.

Two limits remain important. Pellet-scale dielectric response and electrode-scale electrochemical kinetics are measured over different length scales, so their connection is correlative rather than causal. Operando XAS is also a bulk-averaged probe and cannot isolate a surface-confined reaction.

Within those bounds, the work offers a practical design principle: pair a redox-active perovskite with a high-permittivity insulator, then verify the division of labour spectroscopically rather than inferring it from performance.
