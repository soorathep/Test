A2. Read the axes and the state

We plot g(x) = Δgmix(x)/(RT), a dimensionless molar mixing energy. The horizontal variable x = x₁ describes a possible homogeneous liquid. The overall feed composition is z = z₁.

At a given z, the curve represents one homogeneous liquid. A feasible mixture of two compositions lies on the line segment joining their energies. Compare states at the same z. The affine pure-liquid reference cancels because the total amounts of both components are the same.

At z = 0.12: Mark the homogeneous point, the lower two-liquid point, and the two tangent contacts on the graph. Which state has lower energy? __________________

A3. Keep the two tests separate

TestWhat it establishes
Local curvature g″(z)Positive: stable to sufficiently small composition fluctuations. Negative: locally unstable.
Global energy/support testCompares all allowed states. A supporting common tangent lies on or below the whole curve.

Positive curvature does not rule out a lower-energy distant split. A negative mixing energy does not prove stability against two mixed liquids. All calculations here use a hypothetical binary liquid model; vapor and solids are excluded.

B. Work one case: A = 3, z = 0.12

g(x) = x ln x + (1 − x) ln(1 − x) + 3x(1 − x)
g′(x) = ln[x/(1 − x)] + 3(1 − 2x)
g″(x) = 1/x + 1/(1 − x) − 6

B1. Check local curvature

g″(0.12) = 8.333333 + 1.136364 − 6 = +3.469697.
This is locally stable; the global question is still open.

B2. Locate the common-tangent contacts

The general tangent condition is g′(xα) = g′(xβ) = [g(xβ) − g(xα)]/(xβ − xα). In this symmetric case the tangent is horizontal, so xβ = 1 − xα and g′(xα) = 0.

The low-composition root is between 0.0707 and 0.0708. Refining it gives xα = 0.07072018 and xβ = 0.92927982. Substitute xα into g′ to check a value near zero. The stationary point 0.50 has negative curvature and is not a binodal endpoint.

B3. Calculate amounts and check the balance

Use a basis of 1 mol of total feed. The mole-based phase fractions describe amounts of liquid phases, not compositions or vapor fractions.

fβ = (z − xα)/(xβ − xα)
= (0.12 − 0.07072018)/0.85855964 = 0.057398
fα = 1 − fβ = 0.942602
0.942602(0.07072018) + 0.057398(0.92927982) ≈ 0.120000

Both tangent contacts have g = −0.05834135. Thus gsplit = fαg(xα) + fβg(xβ) = −0.05834135. The decrease is g(z) − gsplit = 0.00821636. Multiply by nRT for a total energy in joules when R is in J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹.

B5. Complete the global test

Check g(x) − ℓ(x) ≥ 0 across the whole interval, where ℓ(x) = −0.05834135. The candidate minima are the endpoints and stationary points: d(0) = d(1) = 0.05834135; d(0.50) = 0.11519417; d(xα) = d(xβ) = 0. All are nonnegative. A coarse plot alone can miss a dip. Conclusion: the homogeneous z = 0.12 liquid is metastable; equilibrium is two liquids.
