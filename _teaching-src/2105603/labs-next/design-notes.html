Instructor design notes

Design observations, the redesign sequence for all 13 labs and the usability-pilot protocol. Students start from the Labs 2027 overview.

What the classroom walkthrough revealed

The 13 November 2026 walkthrough surfaced four questions before the electrochemistry investigation could proceed.

  • Where is the starting example?
  • What do the left and right species tables represent?
  • Which number changes the concentration?
  • What should I try after the first calculation?

The prototype puts the starting case, editable scientific quantities and next actions together. The audit of the other labs identifies similar design risks for review.

A shared structure for the next version

Begin with the assigned case

Use the same names and starting values in the lesson, worksheet and lab. Show the starting case before detailed model settings.

Edit the quantity you understand

Label concentrations, temperature and pressure with their units. Use structured data tables and equation previews for imported measurements.

Connect a prediction to the result

Keep a short prediction near the calculate action. Show the numerical result, its physical meaning and a calculation the student can check.

Keep the comparison together

Save named cases with their inputs and notes. Reopen the study and recalculate it. Present validation messages beside the quantity that needs attention.

Try the prototype with a first-time student

  1. Open the Lab 01 starting problem, predict its phases, and calculate without verbal instructions.
  2. Explain the liquid and vapor compositions and check the component balance.
  3. Save the starting result, raise the pressure to 100 kPa, and explain the change in phase amount.
  4. Download the study, reopen it, and reproduce a saved comparison.

Record time, incorrect edits, places where help is needed, and the student's explanation. Include keyboard use and a narrow screen in the review.

For Lab 02, inspect the starting measurement, check your activity-coefficient calculation, fit 60 kPa, evaluate 80 kPa without refitting, and reopen a saved comparison.

For the graduate routes, ask the student to defend a claim using the sensitivity outputs, explain why the sampled range is not a confidence interval, and reopen the saved inputs and evidence record.

For Lab 03, ask the student to find the error hidden by an area criterion, explain a method marked not assessed, compare an endpoint assumption and reopen the complete diagnostic record.

For Lab 04, ask the student to classify z₁ = 0.12 before calculating, explain why positive curvature did not decide it, rerun the sensitivity at A = 2.05, and name the measurement that separates two models with the same ln γ∞.

For Lab 05, ask the student to predict what a near-zero residual shows, explain why α = 0.47 converges yet misses the tie line, and defend one τ(T) assumption using the held-out errors.

These are design prototypes for Labs 01–08 and 13. Independent student usability sessions and the remaining four lab interfaces are future work. Numerical checks use the existing course calculation engines.
