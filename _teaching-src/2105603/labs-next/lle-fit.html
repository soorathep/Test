Does a perfect fit identify the model?

What is minimized?

For each component i, the residual is ri = ln(xiγi)α − ln(xiγi)β. The objective is the mean of r₁² and r₂². It is unweighted and dimensionless; it is not a maximum-likelihood fit of the measured compositions. A bounded Nelder–Mead simplex stops when its size falls below 10⁻⁸. After fitting, the lab solves the global common tangent of the fitted model and compares the predicted liquids with the data.

Open the current Lab 05 for pasted data, synthetic noise, the objective surface and convergence plots.

    4. Check your understanding

    Write first, then compare

    Use the calculations above to answer each question in your own words. Then open the explanation and compare. Your answers are saved with your study.

    A. Why does the fit reproduce the tie line exactly?

    Fit the measured case at 298.15 K and read the chemical-potential residual and the paper check.

    Compare with the explanation

    The residual is about 10⁻¹⁰. One tie line gives two equations, equal μ₁ and equal μ₂ in both liquids, and the fit has two unknowns, τ₁₂ and τ₂₁, with α fixed. A two-parameter model can usually satisfy two equations exactly.

    So the near-zero residual shows that the tie line was reproduced. It does not test whether NRTL with these τ values describes the mixture at any other condition.

    B. Why can constant τ not follow the measured tie lines?

    Compare the measured x₁α and x₁β at 278.15 K and 316.15 K with the fitted values at 298.15 K.

    Compare with the explanation

    With α fixed, gE/RT depends only on composition and the two τ values. If τ is held constant, the predicted coexisting compositions are the same at every temperature: 0.177 and 0.876.

    The measured gap narrows from 0.104–0.942 at 278.15 K to 0.301–0.702 at 316.15 K, as the system approaches its upper critical solution temperature. The interaction parameters must decrease as T rises, for example τ = a + b/T.

    C. Why is a converged fit not automatically an acceptable one?

    Set α = 0.47, fit the measured tie line at 298.15 K, and read the verdict and the Gibbs plot.

    Compare with the explanation

    At α = 0.47 the optimizer converges, but no τ pair between −2 and 6 makes both chemical potentials equal: the predicted liquids miss the data by about 0.40 in mole fraction.

    Converged only means the search stopped moving. An acceptable fit needs equal chemical potentials at the observed compositions and a global common tangent that puts the predicted liquids at those compositions.

    4. Investigate what the fit can support

    Master's core work

    Fit a tie line in Step 1 before running an investigation.

    What these results can and cannot show

    • One tie line gives two equations for two τ values. A near-zero residual is calibration; it cannot by itself test the model.
    • Temperature transfer depends on an assumed τ(T). Held-out tie lines test that assumption together with the NRTL form and α.
    • The composition half-widths are scenario bounds. Refitted τ ranges are deterministic: they are not confidence intervals, and the source's expanded uncertainties are not used as regression weights.
    • The model is binary and liquid-only. It does not include the vapor phase or pressure dependence.

    A. Separate calibration from validation

    1. Load the measured case at 298.15 K and fit it. Note the residual and the composition error.
    2. Predict every other reported tie line with constant τ and with τ ∝ 1/T. Read the held-out error and how many predictions fall within the expanded uncertainty.
    3. Choose a second calibration temperature and repeat with τ = a + b/T. Explain why two temperatures can fit the trend but a third is still needed to test it.
