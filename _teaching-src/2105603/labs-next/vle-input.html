    A. Read the first row by hand

    Load the example. Use the first-row preview to calculate γ₁ and γ₂ yourself.

    Compare with the explanation

    At 350 K, P₁sat = 150 kPa and P₂sat = 60 kPa. With x₁ = 0.05, y₁ = 0.3975 and P = 95.12 kPa: γ₁ = 0.3975 × 95.12/(0.05 × 150) = 5.04 and γ₂ = 0.6025 × 95.12/(0.95 × 60) = 1.005.

    Component 1 is dilute here, so γ₁ is near its infinite-dilution value, while component 2 is almost pure and γ₂ ≈ 1.

    B. What does a wrong pressure unit look like?

    Set the P column unit to bar (the numbers are really kPa) and validate again. Compare the pure-liquid checks and the fit with the correct import.

    Compare with the explanation

    Every P is multiplied by 100, so both γ values are multiplied by 100 and both ln γ rise by ln 100 = 4.61. ln γ₁ at x₁ = 0.95 becomes 4.61 instead of 0.004, which violates the pure-liquid limit. The NRTL fit misses the pressure by 99%.

    The ratio γ₁/γ₂ does not change, so the area consistency test of Lab 03 gives the same D = 1.16% and cannot see this mistake. Check the units and the limits before testing consistency.

    C. Does a near-perfect fit prove the data are right?

    Fit NRTL, then Margules with one and with two parameters. Compare the pressure errors.

    Compare with the explanation

    NRTL reproduces the pressure to about 10⁻⁶ %, two-parameter Margules to 0.26% and one-parameter Margules to 1.1%. The example was generated by NRTL with τ₁₂ = 0.8 and τ₂₁ = 1.2, which the fit recovers.

    A good fit shows that a model can represent the table. It does not show that the measurements are accurate or thermodynamically consistent; Lab 03 tests consistency separately.

    4. Investigate what each check can catch

    Master's core work

    Validate a table in Step 1 before running an investigation.

    What these results can and cannot show

    • The activity coefficients assume an ideal vapor, negligible Poynting corrections and correct vapor pressures. Their accuracy is limited by your property sources.
    • Endpoint extrapolation is an assumption. It never creates observations, and a full-range area result that depends on it is conditional.
    • A good model fit and a passed consistency test answer different questions; neither certifies measurement accuracy.

    A. Diagnose input mistakes by their signatures

    1. Re-import the same table under four deliberate misreadings: P as bar, P as Pa, T as °C, and Antoine constants with the wrong T unit.

    Model and assumptions

    γi = yiP/(xiPisat) assumes an ideal vapor and negligible Poynting and excess-volume corrections. Saturation pressures come from your Antoine constants at each row's T and are never extrapolated beyond the declared range. Fits minimize the unweighted mean of (100 ΔP/P)² and (100 Δy₁)² with bounded multistart Nelder–Mead.
