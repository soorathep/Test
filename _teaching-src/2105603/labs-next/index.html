What the graduate investigations require

Follow the numbered foundation steps first. Use their results as the control case for the graduate investigations, then complete the evidence record in the same saved study.

InvestigationRun and compareExplain with evidence
Lab 01 · Model and phase robustnessCompare liquid models at the same conditions. Change one assumed input bound at a time, then inspect the joint corner scenarios.Distinguish a robust phase classification from a sensitive phase amount. Identify the assumptions that remain untested and the independent data needed to justify a model.
Lab 02 · Inference and conditional fittingPropagate assumed measurement changes through one activity inference. Compare fixed-α fits using the same training data and retained checking pressure.Separate measurement sensitivity, parameter sensitivity and model discrepancy. Explain how shared measurements correlate inferred activities and what is missing for statistical uncertainty intervals.
Lab 03 · Consistency evidenceCheck method applicability, compare a clean reference with canceling defects, and test sampling, endpoint and measurement assumptions.Explain how methods can disagree, distinguish a criterion from certification, and identify the independent evidence needed for a stronger conclusion.
Lab 04 · Local and global stabilityCompare curvature with the tangent-plane distance for three feeds. Change the model parameter within assumed bounds, including near the critical point, and compare models that share the same dilute limits.Distinguish metastable from stable liquids, separate composition sensitivity from phase-amount sensitivity, and identify the liquid-liquid data that dilute activity coefficients cannot replace.
Lab 05 · Calibration and transferFit two τ values to one tie line, predict the other measured temperatures under three τ(T) assumptions, refit with three fixed α values and within measurement bounds.Separate an exact calibration from held-out evidence, recognise a converged fit that misses the tie line, and compare parameter sensitivity to α, temperature form and composition error.
Lab 06 · Three-phase statesFix the three amounts with an extra specification, sweep the pressure across the three-phase pressure, and vary the volatility ratio.Separate what the phase rule fixes from what the balances leave open, explain the jump in vapor amount at P₃, and identify when the vapor lies inside the liquid gap.
Lab 07 · Energy-closed flashSweep the feed temperature, compare ideal and nonideal liquids at the same feed, and calculate the heater duty for a target vapor amount.Locate where the flash begins, explain the outlet temperature from activity coefficients, and show that T, Q or β each closes the same problem.
Lab 08 · Data before modelsRe-import one table under deliberate unit misreadings, fit three activity models, and compare endpoint assumptions in the area test.Identify which check catches which input mistake, separate model flexibility from data quality, and state what unmeasured endpoints leave conditional.
Lab 09 · Vapor nonidealityDecompose the virial correction to each K-value, compare cross coefficients B₁₂ with the same pure coefficients, and scale B toward the truncation limit.Explain why φᵢ and φᵢˢᵃᵗ partly cancel, separate a combining-rule assumption from mixture data, and state when an ideal vapor is adequate and when an equation of state is needed.

Support each conclusion with at least two numerical comparisons, state the assumptions behind the comparison, and propose an additional measurement that could challenge the claim.

The sampled scenario ranges are deterministic sensitivity results. They do not supply probabilities, confidence intervals or guaranteed bounds. A fixed-α comparison also does not establish parameter identifiability. If checking errors are used to choose α or another model setting, additional independent data are needed to test the selected model.
