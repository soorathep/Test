Caloric model and assumptions

Enthalpies are relative to the pure liquids at 350 K. Cp,1 = 100 and Cp,2 = 120 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ in both phases; Δhvap,1 = 30 000 and Δhvap,2 = 35 000 J mol⁻¹, the same constants as the Lab 01 vapor-pressure laws. The vapor is ideal. Activity parameters are held constant, so the consistent excess enthalpy is zero. Pressure effects on liquid enthalpy, heat loss and shaft work are neglected.

    4. Check your understanding

    Write first, then compare

    Use Steps 1–3 to answer each question in your own words. Then open the explanation and compare. Your answers are saved with your study.

    A. Why is the outlet colder than the feed?

    Calculate the starting case and compare T with TF.

    Compare with the explanation

    The outlet is at 348.47 K, 21.5 K below the 370 K feed. Vaporizing 0.0755 mol needs 0.0755 × 31 951 = 2412 J. With no heat supplied, that energy comes from the stream itself: cooling 1 mol by 21.5 K releases 112 × 21.5 = 2412 J.

    The outlet settles just above the feed's bubble temperature at 90 kPa (347.95 K), where the remaining liquid is saturated.

    B. Why does so little of the feed vaporize?

    Compare the starting case with “Hotter feed · 390 K”. How much more vapor does 20 K of extra feed temperature buy?

    Compare with the explanation

    β rises from 0.0755 to 0.1436 mol. From Cp(TF − T) = β Δhvap, each kelvin of superheat vaporizes only about Cp/Δhvap = 112/32 000 ≈ 0.0035 mol per mol of feed.

    Latent heats are roughly 300 times larger than heat capacities, so an adiabatic flash of a liquid usually vaporizes only a small fraction.

    C. What would it take to reach the Lab 01 state?

    Lab 01 found β = 0.30 at 350 K and 90 kPa for the same feed. Use the energy balance to estimate the feed temperature that would give this state with Q = 0.

    Compare with the explanation

    At 350 K the vapor has y₁ = 0.5556, so Δhvap(y) = 32 222 J mol⁻¹. Then TF = 350 + 0.30 × 32 222/112 = 436 K, outside the 300–390 K property range.

    In practice the same state is reached from a 370 K feed by adding heat: Q = hout − hF = 9667 − 2240 = 7427 J per mol of feed. Specifying T (Lab 01), Q (here) or β (Lab 06) are interchangeable ways to close the problem.

    What these results can and cannot show

    • The caloric model is synthetic: equal liquid and vapor heat capacities, constant latent heats, and zero excess enthalpy because the activity parameters do not depend on temperature.
    • A real mixture with temperature-dependent activity coefficients has hE ≠ 0, which changes the energy balance.
    • The flash is an equilibrium stage. It does not describe the time or the drum size needed to separate the phases.
