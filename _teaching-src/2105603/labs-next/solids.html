Lab 10 · Solid–liquid equilibrium with pure solids

When does a liquid mixture start to freeze?

A liquid mixture of two synthetic compounds is cooled at constant pressure. A melts at 350 K (Δhfus = 12 kJ mol⁻¹) and B at 400 K (Δhfus = 15 kJ mol⁻¹). Each crystallizes as a pure solid, and the liquid is ideal. The feed has xA = 0.50 and is cooled to 340 K.

Find which solid appears, how much, and the composition of the remaining liquid. Then locate the temperature below which no liquid remains.

Model and validity

Each solid is pure (no solid solution, polymorph or compound). The solubility equation neglects the heat-capacity difference between liquid and solid. The liquid model must stay single-phase; a liquid that splits is refused (use Lab 04). Temperatures are limited to 250–450 K. The global state is chosen by comparing the Gibbs energy of every candidate phase set.

    A. Calculate the liquid composition on paper

    For the starting case, use the solubility equation for B to find xA of the liquid. Then find the amount of solid B from a balance on A.

    Compare with the explanation

    ln xB = −(15 000/8.314)(1/340 − 1/400) = −0.796, so xB = 0.451 and xA = 0.549. All of A stays in the liquid, so the liquid amount is L = 0.50/0.549 = 0.911 mol and the solid B is 0.089 mol.

    The liquid composition came from T alone. A feed of 0.45 or 0.30 at 340 K gives the same liquid; only the amount of solid changes.

    B. Which solid appears first, and why?

    Compare the starting case with the A-rich feed. Read the temperature at which the first crystals form.

    Compare with the explanation

    At xA = 0.50, B crystallizes first at 346.7 K, below its own melting point of 400 K. At xA = 0.90, A crystallizes first at 341.3 K, below 350 K.

    Dissolving another component lowers each freezing point. The solid that appears first depends on the feed composition, not only on which pure compound melts higher.

    C. What happens at the eutectic?

    Load “Cooler · 320 K”, then “Below the eutectic · 300 K”. Compare the amount of liquid.

    Compare with the explanation

    At 320 K, 0.26 mol of solid B coexists with liquid of xA = 0.676. The eutectic lies at 319.8 K and xA = 0.677. Below it, the feed is entirely solid: 0.5 mol A and 0.5 mol B.

    At the eutectic, liquid, solid A and solid B coexist. With three phases at fixed pressure there are no degrees of freedom: T and xA are fixed, but the amounts are not, as for the three-phase state in Lab 06. They depend on how much heat has been removed.

    What these results can and cannot show

    • The fusion data are synthetic. Real systems may form solid solutions, compounds or polymorphs that this model cannot represent.
    • Neglecting Δcp is reasonable near the melting point and less so far below it.
    • The activity model was not fitted to SLE data. A model that matches VLE does not guarantee the solid–liquid curves.
