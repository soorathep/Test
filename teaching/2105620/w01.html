Why this course exists

Unread

A result nobody can follow does not enter the record. It is, functionally, not a result.

Unfunded

Proposals, reports, and theses are all the same skill under different constraints.

Unhired

Your writing is the only part of your work most people will ever see.

This course treats writing as a designed object — something with a structure you can inspect, test, and repair.

The 60-second research pitch

Activity 1 · 15 minutes

Everyone speaks. Exactly sixty seconds.
The clock is on screen and I will cut you off.

This is your introduction — and it is also the first thing this course measures.

Four sentences. No more.

Activity 1

  1. Who I am ___, and I work on ___.
  2. The problem The difficulty in this area is that ___.
  3. The attempt What I am trying to do about it is ___.
  4. Why it matters If it works, ___ becomes possible.

Sentence 2 is the one most people cannot finish. Notice that while you listen.

You are not an audience. You are reviewers.

While you listen

Pick any two classmates. For each of them, write down:

  • Could you state their gap in one sentence?
  • Could you state what they will actually do?
  • Mark each: yes / no / almost

Keep the slip. We will look at it again in Week 15.

What just happened

Debrief

  • Sentence 1 was easy Everyone can say what they work on. Topic is not the same as contribution.
  • Sentence 2 was hard Most gap statements came out as “not much work has been done on X.” That is an absence, not a gap.
  • Sentence 3 drifted Several of you described methods instead of an aim. Method is how; aim is what you will settle.
  • Sentence 4 disappeared The significance often vanished under the time limit — exactly as it vanishes under a word limit.

Weeks 5 and 7 are about sentence 2. Week 10 is about sentence 4.

Six genres

Block 3 · 35 minutes

Same findings. Six different documents.
The difference is not style — it is purpose.

What each one is for

The six

Research paper

A new claim, with the evidence that warrants it

Communication

A result too urgent to wait for the full study

Review

A map of what a field currently believes

Data paper

A dataset described so others can reuse it

Technical report

An account for a client or an institution

Thesis

A demonstration that you can do research

Choosing the wrong genre is the most expensive mistake in this list — it wastes months before anyone reads a word.

You can tell the genre without reading the science

Structural evidence

Length 2 500 words says Communication. 14 000 says Review. Nothing else is close.
Reference count Under 40 means original work. Over 150 is almost certainly a Review.
Section headings IMRaD means an argument. Thematic headings mean a survey.
Methods section Absent, or in supplementary material? Communication.
Figure-to-word ratio High and rising · Communication. Low · Review or report.
Front matter Executive summary means an institutional reader, not a referee.

This is the skill Activity 2 asks you to use. It is also the whole of Week 4.

The research paper

Genre 1

  • Makes ONE claim.
  • Everything else in the paper exists to warrant that claim.
  • IMRaD is not a template — it is the shape of an argument: here is the question, here is how I answered it, here is what I found, here is what it means.
  • The Methods section is a promise of reproducibility. It is what separates this genre from journalism.

Typical shape

Words
6 000 – 9 000
Figures
5 – 8
References
40 – 80
Review time
3 – 9 months
Headings
IMRaD

Weeks 5–11 build one of these, section by section.

Two opposite answers to “how much?”

Genres 2 and 3

Communication

  • Speed is the argument — you are claiming this cannot wait
  • Methods usually pushed to supplementary
  • One figure carries the whole claim
  • Rejected hardest for lack of novelty

Review

  • Synthesis is the argument — a new way to organise what is known
  • A list of papers is not a review; it is a bibliography
  • Thematic headings, not IMRaD
  • Usually invited — check before you write one

Both are “a paper.” Neither can be written the way you would write the other.

The three you will also write

Genres 4, 5 and 6

Data paper

The dataset is the contribution. No hypothesis is required — but the description must be complete enough for someone else to reuse it without asking you a question.

Technical report

Written for a client or an institution, not for referees. Executive summary first, because the reader may stop there. Not peer reviewed, and not part of the permanent record.

Thesis

Its purpose is not to report findings but to demonstrate that you can do research. That is why it contains material a journal would cut — failed attempts, full derivations, the reasoning behind choices.

Your thesis is not a stack of papers, and your papers are not chapters. Converting between them is real work.

How credit actually works

Block 5

  • Authorship is earned, not granted Contribution to design, execution, or interpretation — plus drafting, plus approval, plus accountability. Supervising a project is not automatically one of them.
  • Order carries meaning — and it varies First author did the work; last author ran the group. In some fields. Never assume; ask before the first draft, not after.
  • Citation is the currency It is also the audit trail. An uncited idea that is not yours is misconduct, whatever the intention.
  • The record is permanent Retractions stay visible. So does everything you sign your name to.
