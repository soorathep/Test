What can the evidence support?

  1. Write the main finding from the reaction case.
  2. Write one explanation that remains a hypothesis.
  3. Explain the difference to a partner.

The evidence we have

Synthetic means. Residence time 2.0 s, n = 3 per temperature. SD: X = 2 pp, S = 1 pp.

The purpose of the Discussion

  • Answer the research question.
  • Explain what the findings may mean.
  • Compare relevant evidence and alternative explanations.
  • Bound the claims by the study’s limitations.

A Discussion paragraph develops a claim

Claim
The preferred temperature depends on the process objective.

Reasoning
Higher conversion comes with lower selectivity. Both quantities affect the desired-product yield.

Boundary
The present comparison covers four temperatures at one residence time.

A claim and its supporting result

Claim: The preferred temperature depends on the objective.

Evidence: At 375 °C, conversion is highest among the tested conditions, but selectivity is lowest.

Boundary: These measurements alone do not establish an industrial optimum.

Conversion and estimated yield differ

Temperature (°C) Mean conversion (%) Estimated yield (%)
300 40 36.0
325 55 47.3
350 70 54.6
375 80 52.0

Estimated yield = mean conversion × mean selectivity / 100. Synthetic data.

The best tested value has a limited scope

Observed
350 °C gives the highest yield estimate among the four tested temperatures.

Unresolved
Intermediate temperatures may give a different result. Uncertainty must also be considered.

Next decision
Test more temperatures near 350 °C before selecting a condition for further evaluation.

Alternative explanations

  • Could the reaction network change the observed selectivity?
  • Could transport limitations contribute to the trend?
  • Could sampling or analysis bias the measurements?
  • What observation would help distinguish these possibilities?

Evidence that tests an explanation

Possible explanation Useful additional evidence
Changes in product pathways Product distributions across residence times
Transport contributes Response to mixing or particle-size changes
Analysis introduces bias Calibration checks and material-balance closure

Proposed tests for the hypothetical case. None of these outcomes has been measured.

Cautious wording still needs a reason

  • “May reflect competing pathways” states a hypothesis.
  • The next sentence should identify the supporting rationale.
  • A proposed test should show how the hypothesis could be challenged.

Conditions for a fair literature comparison

Which paper would be a fair comparator?

  • Check feed, catalyst, reactor and temperature range.
  • Check the definitions of conversion and selectivity.
  • List the evidence you need before writing a comparison.

A limitation should change the claim

4 setpoints
Limits resolution. Say “highest among those tested” rather than claiming a global optimum.

2.0 s only
Limits generalization. State 2.0 s when describing the temperature response.

Industrial use
Without energy or stability data, the yield estimate remains a screening result.

Discussion connects evidence to a decision

Implication
The conversion–selectivity trade-off makes the choice of temperature depend on the objective.

Support
375 °C gives the highest mean conversion. At 350 °C, the yield estimate from the means is highest.

Boundary
This comparison identifies a condition for further testing. It does not establish an industrial optimum.

Discussion workshop

25 minutes · 10:40–11:05

  1. First 5 min: outline the main claim and the evidence supporting it.
  2. Next 20 min: draft 250–350 words using your Results and figure.
  3. Include an alternative interpretation, a limitation and a test that could distinguish explanations.

The Conclusion answers the original question

  • State the main finding and its magnitude.
  • Give the most defensible implication.
  • Keep the scope tied to the tested conditions.
  • Introduce no new result.

A Conclusion linked to the research question

Main finding
At 2.0 s, raising temperature from 300 to 375 °C increased conversion from 40% to 80% and lowered selectivity from 90% to 65%.

Implication
Conversion alone cannot determine the preferred operating condition.

Next step
Testing near 350 °C could refine the yield comparison within this hypothetical system.

Conclusion workshop

12 minutes · 11:13–11:25

  1. Write 80–120 words answering the original research question.
  2. Include the central numerical comparison and the tested conditions.
  3. Check every claim against Results. Remove new results and unsupported promises.

Peer review of the argument

15 minutes · 11:25–11:40

  1. Underline each paragraph’s claim and mark the evidence supporting it.
  2. Circle any untested explanation stated as fact.
  3. Suggest two edits: one to strengthen the reasoning and one to narrow the scope.

A revision that changes the claim

Before

“The optimal industrial temperature is 350 °C.”

After

“Among the tested temperatures, 350 °C gave the highest yield estimate calculated from the reported mean conversion and selectivity.”

Final revision and submission

15 minutes · 11:45–12:00

  1. Submit Results, figure, caption, Discussion and Conclusion.
  2. Show one revision that better matches the evidence.
  3. Propose the next experiment and an outcome that would challenge your explanation.

Writing guidance

  • Journal instructions determine section structure.
  • Check whether Results and Discussion are separate or combined.
  • Use the target journal’s instructions before submission.
