A literature review is not a pile
of papers you have read.

It is a map of claims,
and the evidence for each one.

Week 1: a list of papers is a bibliography. Today you build the thing that makes it a review.

What the map buys you

Why bother

You stop re-reading

A paper you have mapped is a row you can consult. A paper you have only read is one you will open again in six months and re-read from the start.

Disagreements surface

Two rows, one column apart. This is the single most common source of a real research question.

Empty columns are findings

If nobody reports a quantity, that is not a hole in your reading. It is a hole in the field.

All three of these are invisible when your literature lives in a folder of PDFs named paper1.pdf.

The search

Block 2 · 22 minutes

A search is not something you do. It is something you write down, so that somebody else can run it and get your number.

Three blocks, joined by AND

Structure before syntax

  1. Material or system What the work is made of, or acts on — the anode, the membrane, the catalyst, the feedstock
  2. Process or phenomenon What happens — dendrite growth, deactivation, fouling, selectivity, corrosion
  3. Property or metric What is measured — cycle life, coulombic efficiency, conversion, permeance

Inside a block, synonyms joined by OR. Between blocks, AND. Almost every chemical-engineering question decomposes this way.

The synonym you did not think of

Where searches fail

  • Spelling sulfur / sulphur · aluminum / aluminium · these are different searches
  • The older name What the material was called before 2015. Reviewers who are older than you use it
  • Abbreviation and expansion Both. “SEI” and “solid electrolyte interphase” do not retrieve the same set
  • Trade and systematic names Nafion and perfluorosulfonic acid. One is in the title; the other is in the methods

Forty hits and an empty field is almost never an empty field. It is a missing synonym — and a gap found that way is an artefact of your search.

Four operators, and where they apply

Syntax

AND OR NOT Use NOT sparingly. It removes papers you have not seen, and it removes them silently
"exact phrase" Quotes make a phrase one unit. Without them you get the words anywhere, in any order
dendrit* Truncation catches dendrite, dendrites, dendritic. Truncating too early catches too much
Field limits Title / abstract / keyword. The single most effective way to cut a result set without losing relevance

Syntax differs between databases. What does not differ is that you must record which one you used.

Write the search down like a result

The log line

Scopus · searched 20 Aug 2026 · TITLE-ABS-KEY( (“zinc anode” OR “Zn anode”) AND (dendrit* OR “dendrite suppression”) AND (“cycle life” OR “coulombic efficiency”) ) · 2015–2026 · articles and reviews only · 412 hits

Five things, every time: database · date · the full string · the filters · the number of hits.

If you cannot produce this line, your review has no Methods section — and a review without one is an opinion.

Build a search you could publish

Activity 1 · 15 minutes · your own topic

Eight minutes building and running.
Seven minutes reporting.

Use your own research question. Not a made-up one.

What to do

Activity 1 · 8 min building, 7 min report

  1. Split Your question into three blocks. Material · process · metric
  2. List synonyms At least three per block. Include the ones you would not use yourself
  3. Run it Record the hit count
  4. Refine once One filter only — date, document type, or title/abstract/keyword. Record the new count

Then write the log line. That is what you report.

Folders and labels are not the same thing

Move 3 · organise

Collections

Folders. A paper sits in one. Organise these by project — one per chapter, one per manuscript.

Tags

Labels. A paper can carry five. Organise these by claim type — method, contradicts-X, key, to-read.

Deleting from a collection does not delete the item. That confuses everyone exactly once.

What it costs, and what it does not

Move 6 · sync and share

Free

Data syncing — items, notes, tags, links. Unlimited, on a free account.

300 MB

File storage free, then paid tiers. PDFs are files. This is the limit you will actually hit.

Groups

Free, no limit on members. Only the owner’s quota is charged for group files.

So: sync your library everywhere for nothing. If you also sync every PDF, you will fill 300 MB and have to decide.

One warning about plugins

Before you go looking

Zotero itself does not maintain a plugin list, and its own documentation says plugins have full access to your Zotero and your computer, so install only from developers you trust.

Third-party plugins also break when Zotero updates — and it now ships a new major version every six to ten weeks. Useful ones exist. None of them is required for anything in this course.

Two passes, and the criteria come first

Screening

  1. Write the criteria before you read What makes a paper in-scope. Two or three lines, written down before you open anything
  2. Pass 1 — title and abstract Seconds each. In, out, or unsure. Never read the full text in this pass
  3. Pass 2 — full text, only the survivors And record why each exclusion was excluded, against a criterion

Criteria written afterwards are not criteria. They are a description of what you happened to keep.

The evidence matrix

Activity 2 · 15 minutes · in pairs

Four abstracts. One row each.
Then find where two of them disagree.

The four abstracts were written for this exercise. They are not real papers — do not cite them.

One row per paper

Activity 2 · 8 min filling, 7 min report

Claim What the paper says is true — in one sentence, not a summary
System Material, electrolyte, cell format
Conditions Current density, depth of discharge, temperature, cycle count
Metric The number, with its units. If none is given, write none
Limitation What the authors themselves admit

Then: where do two rows disagree — and what single experiment would settle it?

Then: what your matrix is actually telling you

Three gaps, three shapes

Reading the matrix

Two rows contradict An unresolved question “Reports disagree on whether … under X the effect is …, whereas under Y …”
A column is empty for every row Nobody has measured it “No study to date reports … under … conditions”
Every row shares one condition The field has tested one regime only “Existing work is confined to …; behaviour under … remains uncharacterised”

Each of these is a sentence you can defend, because the table is the evidence for it.

 

“Not much work has been done on X”
is none of those three.

That is an absence.
A gap is what the absence costs us.
