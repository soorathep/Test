Lab 11 · Gas adsorption equilibrium

Where does the gas go?

A rigid 1.0 L vessel holds 20 g of a synthetic adsorbent and 0.030 mol of a single gas at 300 K. Without the adsorbent the pressure would be 74.8 kPa. The adsorbent follows a Langmuir isotherm with qs = 2.0 mol kg⁻¹, b = 0.10 kPa⁻¹ at 300 K and an isosteric heat of 25 kJ mol⁻¹.

Find the equilibrium pressure and how much gas sits on the solid. Then predict what heating the closed vessel does.

Model and validity

Absolute uptake of one gas, ideal gas phase, adsorbed-phase volume neglected, constant isosteric heat and qs independent of temperature. Pressures are limited to 200 kPa. The equilibrium is unique because both the gas and the adsorbed amounts increase with pressure.

    A. Check the material balance

    For the starting case, take the calculated pressure and verify n = PV/RT + m q(P).

    Compare with the explanation

    At P = 14.99 kPa, the gas holds (14 990 Pa)(0.001 m³)/(8.314 × 300) = 0.0060 mol. The uptake is q = 2.0 × 1.499/2.499 = 1.20 mol kg⁻¹, so the solid holds 0.020 kg × 1.20 = 0.0240 mol. The sum is 0.0300 mol.

    80% of the gas is on the solid, and the pressure is 15.0 kPa instead of 74.8 kPa. The coverage θ = 0.60 is far from the low-pressure limit.

    B. Why does a linear isotherm give the wrong pressure?

    Load “Linear isotherm, same initial slope” (K = qsb = 0.2) and compare the pressure.

    Compare with the explanation

    The linear isotherm gives 6.8 kPa and q = 1.36 mol kg⁻¹, against 15.0 kPa and 1.20 mol kg⁻¹ for Langmuir. Both agree only as P → 0.

    At θ = 0.6 the sites are filling, so each extra kPa adds less uptake. A linear isotherm ignores this saturation, overestimates what the solid can hold, and so underestimates the pressure by more than half.

    C. What does heating the closed vessel do?

    Load “Heated to 340 K”. Compare the pressure with the starting case and with an empty vessel heated by the same amount.

    Compare with the explanation

    The pressure rises from 15.0 to 30.3 kPa, a factor of 2.0. An empty vessel would rise only by 340/300 = 1.13, from 74.8 to 84.8 kPa. The solid releases 0.0047 mol, and the gas share rises from 20% to 36%.

    Adsorption is exothermic, so b falls with temperature: b(340 K) = 0.1 × exp[(25 000/8.314)(1/340 − 1/300)] = 0.031 kPa⁻¹. This is the basis of temperature-swing regeneration.

    4. Investigate the isotherm evidence

    What these results can and cannot show

    • The isotherm data are synthetic: a Langmuir isotherm with a fixed ±2% pressure scatter. Real data may need heterogeneous or multisite models.
    • A good fit inside the measured range does not justify the model outside it.
    • The isosteric heat assumes an ideal gas and absolute uptake. Excess uptake must be converted first.
