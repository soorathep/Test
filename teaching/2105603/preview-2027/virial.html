When is an ideal vapor good enough?

Model and validity

The pressure-form second-virial vapor: Z = 1 + BmixP/RT with Bmix = y₁²B₁₁ + 2y₁y₂B₁₂ + y₂²B₂₂. The liquid reference includes φᵢˢᵃᵗ consistently, so B = 0 recovers the ideal vapor exactly. The lab refuses states with max|Bᵢⱼ| · max(P, Pᵢˢᵃᵗ)/RT > 0.1, where neglected higher virial terms may matter; passing this guard is not an accuracy guarantee. Saturation pressures are the Lab 01 synthetic correlations (150 and 60 kPa at 350 K).

    A. What does Z = 0.996 mean?

    Calculate the starting case and read Z. Compute Bmix at the vapor composition and check Z yourself.

    Compare with the explanation

    At y₁ = 0.6148, Bmix = 0.378(−120) + 2(0.6148)(0.3852)(−90) + 0.148(−180) = −114.7 cm³ mol⁻¹. Then Z = 1 + (−114.7 × 10⁻⁶)(95 000)/(8.314 × 350) = 0.9963.

    A negative B means net attraction between molecules: the vapor occupies about 0.4% less volume than an ideal gas at the same T and P.

    B. Why does the result change so little?

    Compare the bubble pressure and β of the two vapors, and read the correction terms in the right-hand figure.

    Compare with the explanation

    The bubble pressure falls from 105.00 to 104.85 kPa (0.14%) and β from 0.494 to 0.488. From yᵢφᵢP = xᵢγᵢφᵢˢᵃᵗPᵢˢᵃᵗ, K₁ changes by the factor φ₁ˢᵃᵗ/φ₁ = exp(−0.0062 + 0.0033) = 0.997.

    Both the vapor mixture and the pure saturated vapor in the reference are nonideal in the same direction, so their corrections largely cancel. At low pressure, |B|P/RT is below 1%, and the ideal vapor is a good approximation.

    C. When does the vapor correction matter?

    Load “Ten times larger |B|” and compare Z, β and the bubble pressure with the ideal vapor.

    Compare with the explanation

    With B ten times larger, Z = 0.963, β falls from 0.494 to 0.438 and the bubble pressure is 1.5% below the ideal-vapor value. The size indicator |B|P/RT reaches 0.093, close to the 0.1 limit of the second-virial truncation.

    Large |B| (strongly attracting or associating vapors such as carboxylic acids) and higher pressure make the vapor correction important. Beyond the limit, an equation of state is needed.

    What these results can and cannot show

    • The coefficients are synthetic and constant with temperature. Real B values depend strongly on T and should come from measurements or a validated correlation.
    • The pressure-form truncation is a low-density approximation. Passing the 0.1 guard does not prove that higher virial terms are negligible.
    • B₁₂ is an independent property of the pair. A geometric-mean or other combining rule is an assumption, not a measurement.
