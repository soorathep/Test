Model assumptions

The liquid model gives the dimensionless mixing Gibbs energy g = ΔmixG/(nRT) at one fixed temperature and pressure. Pure liquids are the standard states. The model contains no vapor phase, interfacial energy, nucleation barrier or kinetics, so a metastable feed can persist in practice even though the split has lower Gibbs energy.

Parameters are hypothetical teaching values unless imported from a fit. No physical temperature is assigned to A.

    4. Check your understanding

    Write first, then compare

    Use the calculations above to answer each question in your own words. Then open the explanation and compare. Your answers are saved with your study.

    A. Why does z₁ = 0.12 split even though its curvature is positive?

    Load the dilute feed, calculate, and read g″(z₁), the minimum TPD and the Gibbs plot.

    Compare with the explanation

    g″(0.12) = 1/(0.12 × 0.88) − 6 = 3.47 > 0, so the liquid resists small composition fluctuations. The minimum tangent-plane distance is −0.245, at a trial liquid near w₁ = 0.95: a liquid far from the feed lies below the tangent at the feed.

    The equilibrium is two liquids (x₁ = 0.0707 and 0.9293). The homogeneous feed is metastable. Curvature is a local test; the tangent-plane distance is a global one.

    B. At A = 3, which results change between z₁ = 0.12 and 0.50?

    Calculate both named feeds and compare x₁α, x₁β and fβ.

    Compare with the explanation

    x₁α = 0.0707 and x₁β = 0.9293 stay the same; fβ rises from 0.057 to 0.50.

    As in Lab 01, a binary with two coexisting phases at fixed T and P has no remaining freedom in the phase compositions. The feed only sets the amounts, through the lever rule fβ = (z₁ − x₁α)/(x₁β − x₁α).

    C. Why can no liquid split occur when A ≤ 2?

    Set A = 2 and then A = 1.5, calculate z₁ = 0.50, and look at the curvature plot.

    Compare with the explanation

    1/[x₁(1 − x₁)] has its minimum value, 4, at x₁ = 0.5. So g″ ≥ 4 − 2A, which is never negative when A ≤ 2. The Gibbs curve is convex everywhere and no common tangent can touch it twice.

    A = 2 at x₁ = 0.5 is the critical point, where the two coexisting compositions merge.

    4. Investigate what decides the split

    Master's core work

    Calculate a feed in Step 1 before running an investigation.

    What these results can and cannot show

    • The model decides equilibrium at fixed T and P. It does not predict whether or how fast a metastable liquid separates, because it has no nucleation barrier, interfacial energy or kinetics.
    • The half-widths you enter are assumed scenario bounds, not measured uncertainties. Each sampled range is deterministic: it is not a probability, a confidence interval or a guaranteed global bound.
    • Models matched at infinite dilution are hypothetical comparisons. Agreement between them would not validate either one; disagreement shows what the dilute-limit data cannot decide.
    • This is a binary analysis. A third component or a vapor phase needs a multicomponent stability test (Lab 06 treats VLLE).
