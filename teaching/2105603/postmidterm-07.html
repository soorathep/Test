Which fraction of the feed becomes vapor?

At 350 K, an ideal binary mixture has P_1^{\rm sat}=150 kPa and P_2^{\rm sat}=60 kPa. The feed contains z_1=0.40 and the pressure is 90 kPa.

  1. Use P=x_1P_1^{\rm sat}+(1-x_1)P_2^{\rm sat} and y_1=x_1P_1^{\rm sat}/P to find the liquid and vapor compositions.
  2. Find the mole fraction of the feed that is vapor, \beta=(z_1-x_1)/(y_1-x_1).
  3. Open Teaching binary · synthetic in Lab 01. Its vapor pressures are supplied by the lab. Select the ideal liquid, set the feed and conditions above, and select Calculate equilibrium. Verify the overall composition on paper.

What if the compositions came from an experiment?

Suppose the measured values at 350 K are x_1=1/3, y_1=5/9 and P=90 kPa. Assume ideal vapor, negligible Poynting correction, and a pure-liquid standard state.

  1. Use \gamma_1=y_1P/(x_1P_1^{\rm sat}). Compare the values inferred using 150 and 120 kPa for P_1^{\rm sat}. Which measured quantity changed?
  2. Before choosing an Antoine correlation, identify its log base, temperature and pressure units, and valid temperature range.
  3. A model fits these data closely. What additional evidence would you seek across compositions to assess thermodynamic consistency?

Which fraction forms the second liquid?

Now consider a different binary mixture. The one-parameter Margules model has A=3 and the overall mole fraction is z_1=0.18.

  1. In Lab 04 choose Margules · 1 parameter, enter A=3 and z_1=0.18, then select Find equilibrium. Read both liquid compositions from the common tangent.
  2. Use the lever rule to calculate the mole fraction of the feed in the liquid richer in component 1. Reconstruct the feed from the two endpoint compositions.
  3. Compare the common tangent with the homogeneous curve at z_1=0.18. Which state has lower Gibbs energy?

Can the vessel reach 10 kPa?

A closed vessel contains 0.0086712 mol of A at 300 K. The free gas volume is 1.00 L. Treat the gas as ideal and use R=8.31446 L kPa mol^{-1} K^{-1}.

Vessel A. Pure solid A can sublime. Its sublimation pressure is 10.0 kPa at this temperature. Calculate how many moles are needed in the gas to reach this pressure. How many remain as bulk solid?

Vessel B. The inventory of A can occupy the gas or adsorb onto 10.0 g of a separate adsorbent. Use absolute Langmuir uptake q=q_s bP_A/(1+bP_A), with q_s=2.00 mol kg^{-1} and b=0.100 kPa^{-1}. Try P_A=5.00 kPa. Does the inventory close?

Which electrode would gain metal?

At 298.15 K, both electrodes use \mathrm{M^{2+}/M}. Take oxidation on the left, reduction on the right, and zero junction potential. If the circuit is closed, deposition can occur at the electrode undergoing reduction. Use a_L=0.040 and a_R=0.40.

  1. Write the reaction, keeping each species in its own compartment. Which ion activity belongs in the numerator of Q?
  2. The standard cell potential is zero. What is K? Compare Q with K to predict the direction and which electrode gains metal.
  3. Calculate E=E^\circ-(0.05915935\ {\rm V}/n)\log_{10}Q and \Delta_rG=-nFE. Use n=2 and F=96485 C mol^{-1}.
