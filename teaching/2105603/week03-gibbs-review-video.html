First, separate consistency from stability. Lab zero three asks whether data are compatible with an applicable thermodynamic constraint, such as Gibbs Duhem, under the assumptions of the test. Lab zero four asks which feasible state has the lowest total Gibbs energy. These are different questions. A consistent liquid model can predict separation into two liquids. Slide 4: Read the axes before the curve

Recording 2:14 | Optional Gibbs and stability review

Read the axes before interpreting the shape. The horizontal coordinate, x, is the mole fraction of component one in a possible homogeneous liquid. The vertical coordinate, g, is the molar Gibbs energy of mixing divided by R T. It is dimensionless. The symbol z describes the overall feed composition. One liquid has x equal to z, but two liquids can have different compositions whose weighted average is z. We compare states containing the same total amounts of both components. Their pure-liquid reference contributions then cancel. In guide A two, mark the feed at zero point one two.

Slide 5: Compare states at the same overall composition

Recording 3:02 | Optional Gibbs and stability review

At the marked feed, the point on the curved line represents one homogeneous liquid. The point directly below it, on the common-tangent segment, represents a mixture of two liquids. Our stability question compares the homogeneous liquid with other feasible liquid states at the same overall composition.
