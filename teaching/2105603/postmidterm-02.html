1. Composition and phase amount · 10–15 min

Your action: Read this slide only. No worksheet answer is required yet. The next slide starts page 1, Section A.

Symbol Meaning
zᵢ Overall mole fraction in the feed
xᵢ Mole fraction in the liquid phase
yᵢ Mole fraction in the vapor phase
β = V/F Fraction of total moles in the vapor phase

z_i=(1-\beta)x_i+\beta y_i

For each existing phase, its mole fractions sum to one.

2. Starting from Raoult’s law · 35–39 min

Your action: Read this slide. On scratch paper, calculate 60/48 before advancing. No new worksheet entry is required.

For an ideal liquid mixture with ideal vapor:

y_iP=x_iP_i^{sat}(T)

The liquid mole fraction scales the pure-component vapor pressure.

At x₁=0.4 and P₁ˢᵃᵗ=120 kPa, this predicts a partial pressure of 48 kPa.

Suppose the observation instead gives y₁P=0.6(100)=60 kPa. The ideal-liquid prediction is too low.

Think: what factor would multiply 48 to give 60?

A new quantity: activity coefficient · 39–42 min

Your action: Read this definition of gamma. Compare 1.25 with your scratch calculation. No Lab or worksheet entry is required.

The factor is 60/48=1.25. We call it the activity coefficient, written γ (gamma).

y_iP=x_i\gamma_iP_i^{sat}(T)

  • γᵢ=1 recovers Raoult’s law for the liquid.
  • γᵢ>1 gives a higher partial pressure than the ideal-liquid prediction.
  • γᵢ<1 gives a lower partial pressure under the same assumptions.

We still assume ideal vapor and negligible Poynting correction. Verify the observations and vapor-pressure inputs before attributing a mismatch to liquid nonideality.

Activity and its reference · 42–45 min

Your action: Read this slide and check 1.25 × 0.4 on your calculator. Keep worksheet B blank until minute 48.

a_i=\gamma_i x_i

Activity aᵢ represents liquid composition adjusted for nonideal behavior. Here the reference is the pure liquid. Both aᵢ and γᵢ are dimensionless.

For an ideal liquid solution, γᵢ=1 and aᵢ=xᵢ.

For x₁=0.4 and γ₁=1.25, a₁=0.50.

Calculating gamma from VLE · 45–48 min

Your action: Read the equation and assumptions here. You will use them in worksheet page 1, B1–B3 on the next slide.

Rearrange the modified Raoult equation to estimate gamma from an observation.

With ideal vapor and negligible Poynting correction:

y_iP=x_i\gamma_iP_i^{sat}(T)

\gamma_i=\frac{y_iP}{x_iP_i^{sat}(T)}

Use the same pressure units in P and Pˢᵃᵗ. The inferred γ depends on the vapor-pressure correlation and these assumptions.

Antoine check · 82–90 min

Your action: Check page 2, C1–C4 with this slide. Write corrections and their reasons before taking the break.

4-\frac{1000}{50+200}=0,\qquad P^{sat}=10^0\ \mathrm{kPa}=1\ \mathrm{kPa}

  • The equation expects °C. Convert 323.15 K to 50 °C first.
  • A change of log base requires corresponding constants. At this particular zero exponent, both bases happen to give 1, so this single point cannot detect the mistake.
  • 100 °C lies outside the stated valid range.
