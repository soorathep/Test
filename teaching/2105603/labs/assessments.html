1. VLE and flash

Open core lab · Session resources and companion labs

Investigation

Predict the phase before running Lab 01. Export a two-phase state, change pressure at fixed T and z, and verify both component balances. In Lab 09 compare zero Bij with one supported nonzero set; report the coefficient units and guard.

Independent calculation

Independent checkpoint: ideal binary at fixed T, Psat1=150 kPa, Psat2=60 kPa, z1=0.5 and P=90 kPa. Calculate bubble pressure at x1=z1, dew pressure at y1=z1, x1, y1 and vapor fraction. These are specified synthetic vapor pressures, not a property correlation.

Exit explanation

Explain why fixed-temperature and adiabatic flash need different input data.

Evidence: prediction before calculation; saved inputs with model and property sources; baseline and changed case; numerical balance or limiting-case check; supported conclusion and limitation.

2. Data, consistency and model evidence

Open core lab · Session resources and companion labs

Investigation

Load the explicitly synthetic VLE example in Lab 08 and validate it. Fix an interleaved 20% holdout, seed 42, before comparing Margules 1, Margules 2 and NRTL (alpha=0.3). Export training and held-out errors. Run 40 pairs-bootstrap resamples for NRTL and explain the nearly zero spread. In Lab 03 compare a clean case with a controlled defect using an area and a local diagnostic.

Independent calculation

Independent checkpoint: for an ideal vapor at T=350 K, P=100 kPa, x1=0.4, y1=0.6, Psat1=150 kPa and Psat2=60 kPa, compute both activity coefficients. Explain what changes if the Psat pressure unit was mislabeled.

Exit explanation

Why does the model with the smallest training error not automatically win? Why is a consistency diagnostic different from held-out prediction?

3. Stability, LLE and parameter spread

Open core lab · Session resources and companion labs

Investigation

Use Lab 04 to inspect global common-tangent support. In Lab 05 select synthetic replicates, noise amplitude 0.002, seed 42, alpha=0.3 and multistart. Fit and run 40 paired bootstrap resamples. Report parameter and endpoint intervals, failed fits and bound hits. Repeat with alpha=0.4 as a sensitivity study.

Independent calculation

Independent checkpoint: a supported tie line has xAlpha=0.1 and xBeta=0.9. For z1=0.3 compute both liquid fractions and check the balance. Repeat for z1=0.6 at the same T and P.

Exit explanation

Explain why resampling a single measured tie line does not determine empirical uncertainty, and why equal chemical potentials alone do not prove global stability.

Evidence: prediction before calculation; saved inputs with model and property sources; baseline and changed case; numerical balance or limiting-case check; supported conclusion and limitation.

5. Chemical equilibrium and routes to K

Open core lab · Session resources and companion labs

Investigation

Use Lab 12 to compare thermodynamically reconciled routes at the reference T, then vary T to compare constant enthalpy and heat-capacity corrections. Vary pressure at fixed T and distinguish K from equilibrium composition. Export one extent and its balance checks.

Independent calculation

Independent checkpoint: A(g) ⇌ B(g), deltaG°=−5.000 kJ/mol at 298.15 K, ideal gases with the same 1 bar standard. Initially 1 mol A, no B. Find ln K, K and equilibrium extent. Reverse the reaction and double its coefficients in separate comparisons.

Exit explanation

6. Electrochemical equilibrium

Open core lab · Session resources and companion labs

Investigation

In Lab 13 use the concentration cell. Exchange activities, set them equal, then scale both half-reactions consistently. Export the cases and distinguish reversible potential from voltage under load.

Independent calculation

Independent checkpoint: the same M2+/M reduction couple appears on both sides, T=298.15 K, aLeft=0.0100 and aRight=1.000, with equal E°. Calculate Eright−Eleft and deltaG per mole of the balanced cell reaction (n=2). Reverse activities, then double the reaction basis. Neglect junction potential.

Exit explanation

Why must reference electrodes, temperature and activity conventions be consistent?
