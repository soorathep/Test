Week 4 process What controls equilibrium?
Bulk sublimation A separate pure solid can coexist with its gas. While solid remains, P_A=P_{\mathrm{sub}}(T) in this model.
Surface adsorption Molecules occupy an adsorbent surface. An isotherm gives uptake q(P_A) per mass of adsorbent.

Both calculations also need an inventory balance. These conditions answer different questions.

Reaction extent keeps the atoms balanced

Read with worksheet p. 2, A1. Use the same synthetic reaction throughout this lesson. You will complete A1-A3 at slide 8.

\mathrm{A_2(g)}\rightleftharpoons 2\mathrm{A(g)}

Initially: 1 mol of A_2, no A and no inert gas. A is a formal teaching species.

n_{A_2}=1\ \mathrm{mol}-\xi,\qquad n_A=2\xi,\qquad 0\leq\xi\leq1\ \mathrm{mol}

Define the dimensionless fraction f=\xi/(1\ \mathrm{mol}). Then n_{\mathrm{total}}=(1+f) mol.

The conserved atom amount is 2n_{A_2}+n_A=2 mol of A atoms.

Activities make Q dimensionless

Read with worksheet p. 2, A2. The Lab 12 extent calculation assumes one ideal gas phase at fixed temperature and total pressure.

For each gas, a_i=y_iP/p^\circ, with p^\circ=100 kPa.

Q=\frac{a_A^2}{a_{A_2}}=\frac{y_A^2}{y_{A_2}}\frac{P}{p^\circ}

  • P is the actual total pressure.
  • p^\circ defines the standard state and stays fixed in our comparisons.
  • Q describes a composition. K(T) describes equilibrium for the reaction and standard states as written.

Use dimensionless P/p^\circ, not a pressure carrying units inside a logarithm.

Which way does the reaction proceed?

Pause for 6 minutes. Worksheet p. 2, B1. Calculate the driving force at each trial Q, then predict forward or reverse reaction. Check on slide 14.

At 350 K, the supplied standard reaction Gibbs energy is \Delta_rG^\circ=-4000 J/mol of reaction.

\Delta_rG=\Delta_rG^\circ+RT\ln Q

Evaluate Q=1 and Q=10, using R=8.314462618 J mol^{-1} K^{-1}.

Negative \Delta_rG favors increasing extent. Positive \Delta_rG favors decreasing extent. An interior equilibrium has \Delta_rG=0.

Route 1: K from standard Gibbs energy

Read with worksheet p. 3, B2. Write the substitution now. Complete the numeric comparison at slide 13.

\ln K=-\frac{\Delta_rG^\circ}{RT}

At 350 K, use \Delta_rG^\circ=-4.000 kJ per mol of the reaction as written.

Convert kJ to J before using R=8.314462618 J mol^{-1} K^{-1}.

Calculate \ln K first, then K=\exp(\ln K). A standard Gibbs energy supplied at one temperature is a point value.

Route 2: K from enthalpy and entropy

Read with worksheet p. 3, B3. Keep the same reaction, temperature and standard states as Route 1.

At the reference temperature 350 K, the synthetic definitions are:

\Delta_rH^\circ=50000\ \mathrm{J/mol},\qquad \Delta_rS^\circ=\frac{54000}{350}\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}

\Delta_rG^\circ=\Delta_rH^\circ-T\Delta_rS^\circ

Then use the same \ln K=-\Delta_rG^\circ/(RT) relation.

An endothermic reaction can have K>1: the entropy term also contributes to the standard Gibbs energy.

Temperature changes the equilibrium constant

Read with worksheet p. 5, E1. Keep P=p^\circ=100 kPa for the next case.

The direct value \Delta_rG^\circ=-4.000 kJ/mol was supplied at 350 K. It does not describe every temperature.

For this lesson, define a constant reaction enthalpy of +50.000 kJ/mol over 350-400 K, equivalent here to \Delta_rC_p^\circ=0.

The positive enthalpy predicts that increasing temperature increases K for the reaction as written.
