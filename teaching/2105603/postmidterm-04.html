Pure-solid standard state

Your action: Read the two phase descriptions. Use them to set up B1, worksheet p. 1.

For component i in a liquid and its present pure solid:

\mu_i^L=\mu_i^S,\qquad \mu_i^L=\mu_i^{L,*}+RT\ln(x_i\gamma_i)

The liquid reference \mu_i^{L,*} is the subcooled pure liquid at the same temperature and pressure.

The present pure solid has unit activity on its pure-solid standard state. Unit activity does not mean zero chemical potential or a zero amount of solid.

x_i\gamma_i specifies a liquid activity. A material balance is still needed to determine phase amounts.

Solubility equation

Your action: Identify the units and assumptions before substituting into B1.

For a present pure solid, with constant fusion enthalpy and negligible fusion heat-capacity correction:

\ln(x_i\gamma_i)=-\frac{\Delta H_{fus,i}}{R} \left(\frac{1}{T}-\frac{1}{T_{m,i}}\right)

Use kelvin, \Delta H_{fus} in J mol^{-1}, and R=8.314462618 J mol^{-1} K^{-1}.

For T<T_m and positive \Delta H_{fus}, the right side is negative. With an ideal liquid, \gamma_i=1 and the result gives x_i^{sat}<1.

This model excludes solid solutions, chemical reactions and heat-capacity corrections. Other solid forms or transitions require additional data.

Sublimation equilibrium

Your action: Read the condition on solid presence. Begin C1, worksheet p. 2 by identifying the gas species.

For a present pure bulk solid A in equilibrium with its vapor:

\mu_A^S=\mu_A^g

With an ideal gas, negligible solid pressure correction, and the stated sublimation-pressure model:

p_A=P_{sub}(T)\quad\text{while solid remains}

p_A is the partial pressure of the subliming species. An inert gas contributes to total pressure.

If all solid disappears, there is no remaining bulk-solid phase to enforce this equality. The finite inventory may give p_A<P_{sub}.

Finite-vessel inventory

Your action: Use this decision sequence for C1–C3. Write the capacity before deciding which phases are present.

For a closed, rigid vessel at fixed T,V_g with ideal gases:

n_g^{sat}=\frac{P_{sub}V_g}{RT},\quad n_A^g=\min(n_A^{tot},n_g^{sat}),\quad n_A^s=n_A^{tot}-n_A^g

p_A=\frac{n_A^gRT}{V_g},\qquad P_{tot}=p_A+\frac{n_{inert}RT}{V_g}

Use R=8.314462618 kPa L mol^{-1} K^{-1} with pressure in kPa and gas volume in L.

For C3, (\mu_A^g-\mu_A^S)/(RT)=\ln(p_A/P_{sub}). A present solid requires zero. An exhausted-solid state may have a negative value.

Adsorption and bulk phases

Your action: Read the definition of uptake before starting E, worksheet p. 4.

Adsorption describes material associated with a surface. An isotherm gives uptake at a specified temperature and gas pressure.

q(P,T)=\frac{\text{moles adsorbed}}{\text{mass of adsorbent}}

Here, q is absolute uptake in mol kg^{-1} and P is the adsorbate partial pressure in kPa.

Adsorbed moles are n_{ads}=m_s q, with adsorbent mass m_s in kg. The symbol q itself is not an amount in moles.

Bulk sublimation and surface adsorption require different state descriptions and inventory balances.

Langmuir model

Your action: Identify the parameters and dimensionless group. Use this expression for E1–E3.

q=q_s\frac{bP}{1+bP},\qquad \theta=\frac{q}{q_s}=\frac{bP}{1+bP}

Quantity Meaning Unit used here
q_s Saturation uptake mol kg^{-1}
b Affinity parameter kPa^{-1}
bP Dimensionless pressure group 1
\theta Fractional coverage in the model 1

The idealized model assumes a finite set of equivalent sites and no lateral interactions. A good fit alone does not establish this microscopic mechanism.

Henry limit

Your action: Derive the low-pressure slope from Langmuir. Retain the units when comparing the models.

If bP\ll1:

q\approx q_s bP=K_H P,\qquad K_H=q_s b

K_H has units mol kg^{-1} kPa^{-1} for this adsorption convention.

The matched Henry line and Langmuir curve share the slope at P\rightarrow0. The Henry line has no saturation plateau and requires a low-pressure domain.

A gas-solubility convention such as p=Hx defines a different constant with different units. State the equation and concentration basis whenever using the name “Henry constant.”

Closed adsorption vessel

Your action: Read the balance before F1–F3, worksheet p. 4. Identify which pressure is unknown.

For an ideal gas and absolute uptake in a closed vessel:

n_A^{tot}=\underbrace{\frac{PV_g}{RT}}_{n_A^g} +\underbrace{m_s q(P,T)}_{n_A^{ads}}

Use the accessible gas volume V_g and adsorbent mass m_s in kg. The unknown P is adsorbate partial pressure.

At fixed T,V_g,m_s,n_A^{tot}, the isotherm and balance determine P together. Both terms increase with P\ge0 for positive Langmuir parameters.

Excess uptake requires a gas-density and volume convention. It cannot be substituted directly for absolute uptake in this balance.

Choosing an equilibrium description

Your action: Use these questions to prepare your reflection in I, worksheet p. 6.

  1. What process occurs? Bulk phase transfer, surface uptake or chemical reaction?
  2. What is fixed? Temperature, pressure, accessible volume and/or species inventory?
  3. Which model and convention apply? Standard states, gas ideality, uptake basis, units and pressure range?
  4. What sets amounts? Combine the equilibrium relation with material balances and check phase presence.

A solubility composition, sublimation pressure or isotherm loading describes part of the equilibrium state. None replaces an inventory balance.
